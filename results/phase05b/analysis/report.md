**Direction of the nested-bootstrap bias, and it points the wrong way for
§7.** The bootstrap means of tau_selfA and tau_selfB sit BELOW their point
estimates. That is expected, not a defect: level 2 of §6.6 resamples
completions, which adds a second layer of sampling noise on top of an
already-noisy P-hat, and extra noise attenuates a reliability coefficient
systematically downward. Because tau_corr divides by sqrt(tau_selfA *
tau_selfB), a downward bias in the denominator produces an UPWARD bias in
resampled tau_corr — visible above as a bootstrap mean above the point
estimate. §7 tests the CI LOWER bound, so this bias is
**anti-conservative**: it makes GO marginally easier to reach than the
nominal 0.30 threshold implies. The procedure is pre-registered and is
not changed here; the direction is stated so a reviewer can weigh a
borderline lower bound correctly.

The tau disattenuation is a heuristic: Spearman's correction is derived for
Pearson r under classical test theory. rho_corr is the formally licensed
version. A material disagreement between the two is itself a finding (§9.4).

The split-half ceiling is the reliability of a k=10 estimate, but tau_cross
uses k=20, so the ceiling is a CONSERVATIVE (low) estimate of the true k=20
ceiling and tau_corr is therefore slightly OVER-corrected (§6.2, §9.3).

## The tau_b tie ceiling

`tau_b = num / sqrt(den_A * den_B)`, where `den_A` counts prompt-pairs
Model A gives different P-hats to. A pair that A **ties** contributes 0 to
the numerator but still sits in `den_B`. So when one model is pinned near
the floor and ties most pairs while the other spreads out, tau_cross is
capped below 1 by tie structure alone — before any question of agreement:

    max tau_cross = sqrt(min(den_A, den_B) / max(den_A, den_B))

**The §6.2 attenuation correction cannot remove this.** Each tau_self
compares two halves of the *same* model, which share a tie structure, so
their own ceiling is ~1.0. The reliabilities are blind to a *between-model*
tie asymmetry, and tau_corr inherits it uncorrected. This is a genuine gap
in §6.2, which is why the ceiling is computed rather than assumed benign.
